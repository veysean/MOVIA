        The problem

        A charger that might be broken is the same as no charger at all.

        At dawn, a rider pulls up to a charging hub on National Road 6A with one question: is it actually working today? He checks two or three Facebook groups before he's sure — and by then he's lost the time he was trying to save. Going electric already cut his fuel cost by more than 70%. The bottleneck was never the vehicle. It's the information.

        Cambodia does not lack EV demand. It lacks trustworthy mobility information.

          Product architecture

          Three layers, not two.

          A direct pipe from riders to partners is the "your data is the product" model — the one that breaks trust. So we put something in between. Raw, identifiable data never leaves the rider layer; only what the system has modeled and aggregated crosses to the paying side.

          Riders are free, always. Partners never see an individual — only what the Trust & Verification Engine has already aggregated and scored.

          What we're building first

          Three features are known. Everything else waits.

          Every feature carries an evidence tag. The ones that generate commission come first, because they need the least data to work: one operator, one rider, one completed charge is already revenue. Retention and infrastructure features wait their turn.
